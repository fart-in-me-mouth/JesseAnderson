The work

A complete philosophy of the natural life, argued from the ground up

The Apexist Summa is a comprehensive philosophical examination, systematization, and defense of the worldview of Goatis, formerly known as Sv3rige. Written by Jesse Anderson, the work develops Goatis’s teachings into an extensive philosophical framework addressing the foundations of knowledge, the nature of reality, human existence, morality, health, society, and religion.

Spanning nineteen books, 165 formal Questions, and more than 1,300 pages, the Summa employs a method inspired by classical scholastic philosophy. Each subject is examined through structured objections, relevant source passages, developed arguments, replies to opposing positions, and reasoned conclusions. The objective is not merely to summarize Goatis’s teachings, but to investigate their philosophical foundations and construct the strongest available arguments in their defense.

The work begins with questions of epistemology and metaphysics, examining the relationship between sensory experience, reason, knowledge, existence, causation, and the natural world. It then develops an account of human nature and practical judgment, exploring instinct, survival, reproduction, moral nihilism, personal freedom, natural hierarchy, and the purposes of human life.

Subsequent books address nutrition, raw animal foods, physical development, exercise, relationships, family, medicine, scientific authority, technology, political institutions, education, and modern civilization. Throughout these discussions, the Summa examines Goatis’s documented positions, including controversial historical claims, while distinguishing his recorded statements from the author’s independent philosophical arguments and the available empirical evidence.

The concluding four books provide an extensive investigation into the existence of God. Drawing upon classical and contemporary philosophy, they examine the cosmological arguments, Aquinas’s Five Ways, divine simplicity, contingency, the principle of sufficient reason, consciousness, moral arguments, fine-tuning, religious experience, miracles, divine hiddenness, and the problem of evil. The work develops a sustained philosophical case for naturalism and life without gods.

The manuscript incorporates extensive primary-source documentation, scholarly references, historical context, and independent philosophical analysis.
